The eight red flags of phishing

Phishing messages try to make you act before you think. These are the signs PhishGuard trains you to look for. One sign is a reason to slow down; two or more and you should report the message.

1. Suspicious sender

The display name says "Microsoft" or your CEO, but the address is a look-alike (micros0ft.com), a free webmail account or a domain with extra words.

2. Urgency or threats

"Within 24 hours", "final notice", "your account will be suspended". Pressure is used so you do not stop to check.

3. Misleading link

The text of a link can say anything. Hover over it (or press and hold on a phone) and read the real address, starting from the main domain just before the first single slash.

4. Risky attachment

Unexpected invoices, .zip or .html files, double extensions like ".pdf.zip", or documents that ask you to "enable editing" or "enable content".

5. Request for passwords or data

No genuine company will ask you to confirm your password, send a one-time code or pay a small "fee" through a link.

6. Impersonated authority

Messages pretending to be from a manager, IT, HR, a bank or the government, because we are used to doing what they ask.

7. Too good to be true

Prizes you never entered, refunds you did not expect, or unusually cheap offers.

8. Generic greeting or poor language

"Dear User", "Dear Customer", odd grammar or spelling. Be careful: well-written phishing is now common, so good grammar proves nothing.

What to do if you are not sure

  • Do not click, reply or open attachments.
  • Check with the sender using a phone number or address you already know, not one in the message.
  • Report it to your IT contact or manager. Reporting quickly protects your colleagues too.
  • If you already clicked or typed a password, tell IT immediately and change the password.
